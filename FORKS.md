# Forks and compatible projects

Forks, modified distributions, commercial products, hosted services, and
compatible implementations are allowed by the MIT license. This guide explains
how to avoid confusing users about official status.

## You may

- fork, modify, redistribute, sell, or host your own version under the
  repository license;
- say that your project is a fork of ClawJS when that is true;
- say that your project is compatible with Claw or ClawJS when the statement is
  truthful and scoped;
- provide your own support, hosting, packages, docs, or integrations under your
  own identity.

## You must avoid confusion

Use a distinct project, package, service, and channel identity for modified or
third-party distributions. Do not imply that your build, package, registry,
service, support channel, or website is official unless upstream maintains or
authorizes it.

Use compatibility language instead of official language:

- Good: "compatible with ClawJS Core contracts"
- Good: "a fork of ClawJS"
- Not OK without permission: "Official ClawJS"
- Not OK without permission: "Claw official build"
